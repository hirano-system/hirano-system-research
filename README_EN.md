## From Man-Month-Based System Development to Success-Fee-Based and Knowledge-Intensive System Development

### Research Overview

This research is based on continuous research into system development since 1986 and examines the transformation from conventional **man-month-based, labor-intensive, multi-tier subcontracting system development** to **success-fee-based, knowledge-intensive system development**.

In conventional system development, the scale of development has generally been calculated in man-months, and the necessary personnel are assigned to build the system. However, as systems have become more complex and IT has become deeply connected with business management itself, it has become increasingly important not only to complete a system but also to clarify what outcomes and profits have been generated by the IT investment.

Therefore, this research has sought to realize success-fee-based system development, in which **IT investment planning, system development, effect measurement, evaluation, contracts, organizations, and risk management** are considered as an integrated whole, and the value of system development is evaluated based on customer outcomes and profits.

Furthermore, by combining **software engineering, management and accounting, contract theory, organizational theory, and AI-based evaluation**, this research develops a theoretical framework for transforming system development from work that simply involves adding people to build systems into a **knowledge-intensive business that accumulates, shares, and reuses knowledge while creating outcomes together with customers**.

This repository publishes the history of the research, research results and theoretical framework, research chronology, and research materials from 1986 to the present.

## Research History

### 1986 — Problem Awareness in the System Development Field

While involved in the design and construction of systems for a securities company, I experienced many problems in system development.

In particular, I became concerned about the dependence on a **"human-wave approach"** in which large numbers of people were assigned to solve problems, as well as the practice of completing development through long working hours.

This experience became the starting point for my subsequent research into system development methodologies.

### 1987–1990 — Work on Software Engineering

As a project leader at a major Japanese computer manufacturer, I was involved in system development including hardware, networks, and software.

During this period, I became aware of the problem that system development planning and estimation depended heavily on individual experience and ability. I studied approaches to **software engineering** developed in the United States and applied them to actual development projects.

I subsequently had opportunities to teach what I had learned about software engineering.

I also experienced planning and project management for outsourcing businesses aimed at reducing system operation costs.

### 1991–1994 — Formation of the Research Framework

From the early 1990s, I began full-scale research into system development.

The research scope expanded to include:

* **Methodologies**
* **Development tools**
* **Technologies that have evolved into today's generative AI**
* **Reengineering**
* **Reusability**
* **Human resources**
* **Management**

At that time, system development involved dependence on individual capabilities, multi-tier subcontracting, and division of development processes. I also experienced cases in which systems did not operate as expected even after they had been built.

In 1994, I organized the research conducted up to that point into a single framework.

The materials from 1994 are important research materials within this research archive.

### 1995–2001 — Development Toward IT Investment Assessment

From 1995 onward, I worked on system planning and strategy at the headquarters of a railway company.

When proposing the introduction of a system, it was important not simply to explain that **"the system is necessary,"** but to explain numerically what effects could be obtained from the IT investment.

Based on this awareness, I continued research into **IT investment assessment**.

While also being involved in planning information strategies for railway and bus operations and group companies using transportation IC cards, I studied methods for evaluating IT investments by combining multiple fields, including:

* **Management**
* **Industrial Engineering (IE)**
* **Accounting**
* **Finance**
* **AI**

This research subsequently led to the concept of the **"Success-Fee-Based Software Development Business."**

### 2001 — Success-Fee-Based Software Development Business

In 2001, after leaving the railway company, I announced a business model called the **"Success-Fee-Based Software Development Business."**

In conventional system development, development costs were generally calculated based on the number of personnel assigned and the development period.

In contrast, the success-fee-based model focuses on **how much the customer's profits and outcomes can be improved through system development**.

In other words:

**Man-months → Outcomes**

In 2001, I presented this business concept at venture-related events.

At that time, the environment surrounding Internet connections, data centers, and system development technologies was not as mature as it is today, and I believed that more time would be required to realize the concept.

Therefore, I continued the research.

### 2001–2018 — Research Toward Realizing Success-Fee-Based System Development

Realizing success-fee-based system development required more than simply changing the contract method.

I therefore concluded that the research itself needed to be reconsidered as an **integrated system** covering:

* **Planning**
* **Requirements definition**
* **Design**
* **Development**
* **Testing**
* **Implementation**
* **Operation**
* **Effect measurement**
* **IT investment assessment**
* **Contracts**
* **Profit allocation**
* **Risk management**
* **Organizations**
* **Human resources**
* **Knowledge management**

A particularly important research question became:

> **How can the outcomes created by IT investment be measured and evaluated?**

### 2017–2018 — Systematization of Theory and Methodology

In 2017, I had an opportunity to become involved in quality improvement at a major software company.

This experience again raised questions concerning the application of software engineering to system development and the quality of development processes.

By 2018, I had systematized a theoretical and methodological framework for realizing success-fee-based system development by combining:

* **System development process theory**
* **Contract theory**
* **System construction technologies**
* **IT investment assessment**
* **AI-based evaluation**
* **The relationship between AI agents and ontologies**
* **Automatic collection and visualization of management information using digital twins**
* **Risk management**
* **Internal and external safety**
* **Organizational theory for promoting new finance and human development**
* **Knowledge-creation organizational theory for human development**

In 2018, I presented this newly systematized research at a facility related to IT and startups in Fukuoka (FGN), with participation from Tokyo connected online.

At that time, the concept of success-fee-based system development and AI-based evaluation had not yet become sufficiently understood.

### 2022 — Success-Fee-Based System Development / IT Investment Assessment Business Plan

In 2022, I reorganized and re-presented the business concept as the:

**"Success-Fee-Based System Development / IT Investment Assessment Business Plan."**

The central concept was:

> **Transformation from man-month-based, labor-intensive development to knowledge-intensive development.**

This requires a process that includes:

* **Planning profits and outcomes from IT investment**
* **Evaluating IT contribution**
* **Verifying effects after system implementation**
* **Confirming recovery of the investment**
* **Determining the allocation of outcomes and profits**
* **Managing risk**
* **Continuous monitoring**

I also discussed the use of **contract theory and game theory** to address the issue of information asymmetry concerning outcomes between customers and developers, determine how profits should be shared, and consider the role of AI.

### Present — Possibilities Enabled by Generative AI

In recent years, software development technologies using **generative AI** have advanced rapidly.

As a result, system development that does not depend solely on the conventional method of increasing the number of people involved in development is becoming increasingly realistic.

By using AI, there is growing potential to integrate:

**Planning → Design → Development → Testing → Operation → Evaluation**

into a unified process and to realize a development model that takes responsibility for solving customer problems and achieving outcomes.

This research views this change as:

> **"A transformation from man-month-based, labor-intensive development to outcome-based, knowledge-intensive development."**

## Main Research Themes

### Success-Fee-Based System Development

A method of considering system development costs not simply in terms of man-months, but in relation to the **outcomes and profits created for customers**.

### IT Investment Assessment

A method for evaluating what effects IT investment has produced on **corporate management, business operations, and profits**.

### System Development Process

A method of treating the entire process from planning through operation and effect measurement as an integrated whole, while managing **quality, cost, time, outcomes, and safety**.

### Contract Theory

Research into how **outcomes, risks, information, and profits** should be shared between customers and system developers.

### Knowledge-Intensive Organizations

The transformation from organizations that increase sales by increasing the number of personnel to organizations that **accumulate knowledge, methodologies, and technologies and create high added value**.

### AI-Based Evaluation

Methods for evaluating IT investments and the effects of system implementation using **AI agents, data analysis, and related technologies**.

### Digital Twins

Methods for digitally modeling corporate activities and systems and using those models for **planning, evaluation, and prediction**.

### Organizations that Create Innovation

The transformation from organizations that simply manufacture systems into organizations that **create new value together with customers**.

## Basic Concept of the Research

This research considers system development not simply as **"software manufacturing,"** but as an activity that creates changes in customers' management and business operations, measures and evaluates the resulting outcomes, and contributes to the growth of **people, organizations, and regions**.

Therefore, realizing success-fee-based system development requires an integrated approach covering:

* **Management**
* **Accounting**
* **Finance**
* **Industrial Engineering (IE)**
* **Contracts**
* **Organizations**
* **Human resources**
* **Risk management**
* **AI**
* **Data**
* **Knowledge management**
