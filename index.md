## About This Research

This website presents the research of **Naohiko Hirano** and **Hirano System Design Office** on the transformation of system development.

The research examines the transformation from conventional **man-month-based, labor-intensive system development** to **outcome-based, success-fee-based, and knowledge-intensive system development**.

The central idea is to consider system development not simply as software manufacturing, but as an activity that creates changes in customers' management and business operations, measures and evaluates the resulting outcomes, and contributes to the creation of business value.

## Central Research Problem

The central research question is:

> **How can the outcomes and profits generated by IT investment be planned, measured, and evaluated, and how can those outcomes be shared between customers and system developers?**

To address this question, the research integrates system development technology with:

- Organizations

## From Man-Month-Based System Development to Outcome-Based Development

In conventional system development, the scale of development has generally been calculated in man-months, with personnel assigned to build the system.

However, as information systems have become increasingly connected with business management itself, it has become important not only to complete a system but also to clarify what outcomes and profits have been generated by the IT investment.

This research therefore seeks to integrate:

**IT investment planning → System development → Effect measurement → Evaluation → Contract → Organization → Risk management**

and to evaluate the value of system development based on customer outcomes and profits.

The research aims to transform system development from work that simply adds people to build systems into a **knowledge-intensive business that accumulates, shares, and reuses knowledge while creating outcomes together with customers.**

# Research History

## 1986 — Problem Awareness in the System Development Field

While involved in the design and construction of systems for a securities company, Naohiko Hirano experienced problems associated with conventional system development.

In particular, he became concerned about dependence on a "human-wave approach," in which large numbers of people were assigned to solve problems, as well as the practice of completing development through long working hours.

This experience became the starting point for subsequent research into system development methodologies.

## 1987–1990 — Software Engineering

As a project leader at a major Japanese computer manufacturer, Hirano was involved in system development including hardware, networks, and software.

He studied software engineering approaches developed in the United States and applied them to actual development projects.

He also experienced planning and project management for outsourcing businesses aimed at reducing system operation costs.

## 1991–1994 — Formation of the Research Framework

From the early 1990s, research expanded to include:

- Methodologies
- Development tools
- Technologies that have evolved into today's generative AI
- Reengineering
- Reusability
- Human resources
- Management

The research addressed problems including dependence on individual capabilities, multi-tier subcontracting, division of development processes, and systems that did not operate as expected after construction.

In **1994**, the research conducted up to that point was organized into a single framework.

## 1995–2001 — Development Toward IT Investment Assessment

From 1995 onward, research expanded into system planning and strategy.

When proposing an IT system, it became important not simply to explain that a system was necessary, but to explain numerically what effects could be obtained from the IT investment.

Research therefore expanded into **IT investment assessment**, combining areas including:

- Management
- Industrial Engineering (IE)
- Accounting
- Finance
- AI

This research subsequently led to the concept of **Success-Fee-Based Software Development**.

## 2001 — Success-Fee-Based Software Development Business

In 2001, after leaving the railway company, Hirano announced a business model called the **Success-Fee-Based Software Development Business**.

The fundamental change was:

> **Man-months → Outcomes**

Instead of determining development costs primarily from the number of personnel and development period, the model focuses on how much customer profits and outcomes can be improved through system development.

The concept was presented at venture-related events in 2001.

## 2001–2018 — Research Toward Realizing Success-Fee-Based System Development

The research showed that realizing success-fee-based system development requires more than changing the contract method.

The research therefore expanded into an integrated system covering:

- Planning
- Requirements definition
- Design
- Development
- Testing
- Implementation
- Operation
- Effect measurement
- IT investment assessment
- Contracts
- Profit allocation
- Risk management
- Organizations
- Human resources
- Knowledge management

A central question became:

> **How can the outcomes created by IT investment be measured and evaluated?**

## 2017–2018 — Systematization of Theory and Methodology

Research was further systematized through the integration of:

- System development process theory
- Contract theory
- System construction technologies
- IT investment assessment
- AI-based evaluation
- AI agents and ontologies
- Digital twins
- Risk management
- Organizational theory
- Knowledge-creation organizational theory

In **2018**, the research was presented at a facility related to IT and startups in Fukuoka, with participation from Tokyo connected online.

## 2022 — Success-Fee-Based System Development / IT Investment Assessment Business Plan

In 2022, the business concept was reorganized and re-presented as the:

**Success-Fee-Based System Development / IT Investment Assessment Business Plan**

The central concept was:

> **Transformation from man-month-based, labor-intensive development to knowledge-intensive development.**

The proposed process includes:

- Planning profits and outcomes from IT investment
- Evaluating IT contribution
- Verifying effects after system implementation
- Confirming recovery of investment
- Determining the allocation of outcomes and profits
- Managing risk
- Continuous monitoring

Contract theory and game theory were also considered in relation to information asymmetry, outcome sharing, profit allocation, and the role of AI.

# Present Research — Generative AI

Generative AI is rapidly changing software development.

The research examines the possibility of integrating:

**Planning → Design → Development → Testing → Operation → Evaluation**

into a unified process.

The objective is to move beyond a development model based primarily on increasing the number of people involved and toward a model that takes responsibility for solving customer problems and achieving outcomes.

This research describes this transformation as:

> **A transformation from man-month-based, labor-intensive development to outcome-based, knowledge-intensive development.**

# Main Research Themes

## Success-Fee-Based System Development

A method of considering system development costs not simply in terms of man-months, but in relation to the outcomes and profits created for customers.

## IT Investment Assessment

A method for evaluating the effects of IT investment on corporate management, business operations, and profits.

## System Development Process

An integrated approach covering planning through operation and effect measurement while managing quality, cost, time, outcomes, and safety.

## Contract Theory

Research into how outcomes, risks, information, and profits should be shared between customers and system developers.

## Knowledge-Intensive Organizations

Research into the transformation from organizations that increase sales primarily by increasing personnel to organizations that accumulate knowledge, methodologies, and technologies and create high added value.

## AI-Based Evaluation

Research into evaluating IT investments and system implementation effects using AI agents, data analysis, and related technologies.

## Digital Twins

Research into digitally modeling corporate activities and systems and using those models for planning, evaluation, and prediction.

## Organizations that Create Innovation

Research into transforming organizations from those that simply manufacture systems into organizations that create new value together with customers.

# Basic Concept of the Research

This research considers system development not simply as **software manufacturing**, but as an activity that:

1. Creates changes in customers' management and business operations.
2. Measures and evaluates the resulting outcomes.
3. Contributes to the growth of people, organizations, and regions.
4. Creates new value together with customers.

Realizing success-fee-based system development therefore requires an integrated approach covering:

**Management × Accounting × Finance × Industrial Engineering × Contracts × Organizations × Human Resources × Risk Management × AI × Data × Knowledge Management**
