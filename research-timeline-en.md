Research Timeline — 1986 to the Present
1986 — Beginning of the Research
Worked on system design and development for a securities company.
Experienced frequent system problems and heavy reliance on labor-intensive development.
This experience raised fundamental questions about the way software systems were being developed in Japan.
1987–1990 — Introduction of Software Engineering
Worked as a project leader for Fujitsu on hardware, network, and software projects at Yamae Hisano.
Observed that planning, estimation, and development depended heavily on individual skills and lacked clear engineering foundations.
Introduced and practiced software engineering approaches developed in the United States.
Conducted research and implementation of software engineering in actual projects.
Later provided software engineering guidance to a researcher from the Chinese Academy of Sciences who was invited to Japan.
1991–1994 — Research into System Development Processes
Continued researching software engineering and system development processes.
Focused on the processes of planning, design, development, testing, implementation, and operation.
Observed that software engineering was still insufficiently researched and implemented across many organizations.
Problems were frequently caused by dependence on individual skills and multi-tier subcontracting.
In some cases, systems were completed but did not operate properly.
Continued the research independently and subsequently taught system development as a part-time lecturer at a technical college.
1995–2001 — IT Investment and Results Measurement
Moved from the developer/vendor side to the user side and worked on system planning and strategy at Nishi-Nippon Railroad.
Recognized that system proposals had to explain their expected results in numerical terms.
While planning a transportation IC card system, began researching methods for evaluating IT investment.
The research covered management, industrial engineering, accounting, finance, and artificial intelligence.
Studied not only IT systems but also actual business operations and management.
This research ultimately led to the concept of a success-fee-based software development business.
2001 — Presentation of the Success-Fee-Based Software Development Business
After leaving Nishi-Nippon Railroad, presented the concept of a success-fee-based software development business at business and venture-related events.
At the time, Internet infrastructure, data-center utilization, and related technologies were not yet sufficiently mature for the concept to be implemented easily.
Changing from man-month-based billing to success-fee-based development required reconsidering the entire process of planning, design, development, and operation.
Effect measurement and evaluation became a major research challenge.
Research on these issues continued from 2001 through 2018.
2017 — Software Quality and Development Practices
Was entrusted with improving software quality at a major Japanese software company in connection with municipal mergers.
Observed that software engineering practices were still not sufficiently adopted.
Manufacturing quality was heavily dependent on subcontractors.
This experience reinforced the need to reconsider software development processes, organizational structures, and quality management.
2018 — Completion of the Theoretical Framework
Completed a framework intended to make success-fee-based system development possible.
The research included:
system development process theory
contract theory
system construction technology
evaluation theory based on AI agents and management digital twins
organizational theory
Presented the framework at a large Fukuoka Growth Next conference, with the Fukuoka and Tokyo venues connected through a network.
At that time, the concept of success-fee-based system development and AI-based evaluation was still difficult for many IT professionals to understand.
2023 — Re-presentation to IT Experts
Presented the research again at a smaller expert-oriented gathering at Fukuoka Growth Next.
The need for such an approach was strongly felt.
However, the research covered a wide range of disciplines and remained difficult to understand as a unified framework.
The Generative AI Era — A New Turning Point
The rapid development of generative AI has significantly changed the possibilities of software development.
AI-assisted software generation is advancing rapidly.
This development raises fundamental questions about labor-intensive, man-month-based software development and traditional development business models.
The research therefore returns to its original question: how should system development move from labor input toward customer value, measurable results, knowledge accumulation, and continuous improvement?
1986–Present — From Man-Months to Customer Value

The research began with practical experience of problems in software development and gradually expanded into a broader framework covering:

Software Engineering → System Development Processes → IT Investment Evaluation → Effect Measurement → Contract Theory → Organization → Knowledge Accumulation and Reuse → AI-Based Evaluation → Success-Fee-Based System Development

The central theme has remained consistent:

How can system development move from charging for the amount of labor invested to creating and sharing measurable value with the customer?

The research continues to examine how system development can evolve from a labor-intensive business into a knowledge-intensive business centered on customer outcomes, accumulated knowledge, evaluation, risk management, and continuous improvement.
